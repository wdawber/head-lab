Helmets

Helmet test methods that reflect real-world impacts and predict risk of different brain injury types — for cyclists, motorcyclists, industrial workers, and beyond.

What we study

Helmets are designed to prevent head and brain injuries, but standards have not been updated for decades and don’t reflect current understanding of brain injury biomechanics. Our research focuses on test methods that accurately represent real-world scenarios and predict risk of different types of brain injury — particularly diffuse injuries driven by head rotation, which conventional tests don’t capture.

Why it matters

Mandatory tests typically use radial impacts onto flat anvils — yet most real-world cyclist, motorcyclist, and industrial head impacts are oblique. Two helmets that both “pass” certification can differ substantially in real-world protection. Better tests give consumers, manufacturers, standard committees, and authorities the evidence to choose, design, and require safer products.

The lab’s contributions have shaped policy and standards: a new headform for assessing head rotation, developed with the CEN/TC158/WG11 committee, is now published in the EN17950 standard. Our work on e-scooter head injury risk has been cited in policy documents in Norway, Sweden, and the UK PACTS report.

How we do it

We actively pursue routes to disseminate findings to a range of stakeholders. Current programmes include:

  • HiPER — a public cycle-helmet rating with results at hiperhelmets.org. Adult ratings published; the Road Safety Trust has provided further funding to extend the rating to children’s helmets.
  • Industrial helmet rating — based on simulated trips and falls and the resulting head impact conditions. 70% of brain injuries in industrial settings are caused by falls and trips, but current standards don’t test for these.
  • Facial impacts in motorcycle incidents — new test methods for protection against facial and basilar skull fractures. Nearly 50% of head impacts in motorcycle collisions are to the face, but facial protection is minimally tested by current standards.
  • Other head-protection projects — including studies on e-scooter falls (cited in policy documents in Norway, Sweden, and the UK), Sikh turban protection in cycling incidents, and earlier work on cycle helmets.

Funded by the Road Safety Trust, Innovate UK, MIPS, Autoliv Research, and the Department for Transport, with collaborations including trauma experts at Imperial’s St Mary’s major trauma centre.
