About the lab

A lab built around one question.

How does mechanical loading injure the brain — and what can engineers, clinicians, and policymakers do about it? Road traffic incidents, sport, falls, blast exposure, surgical interventions, and disease all subject the brain to mechanical forces. HEAD Lab brings together finite-element modelling, impact testing, sensing, and machine learning to predict the consequences of those forces and to design better protection against them.

What we do

Computational and physical models of the brain.

Our research sits across three pillars. We build high-fidelity finite-element models of the brain — used to identify the location of pathology in chronic traumatic encephalopathy and to reveal the biomechanical signature of loss of consciousness. We monitor brain exposure in real-world contexts, from rugby matches to road traffic incidents, using instrumented mouthguards and surrogate head models. And we test the protective devices that sit between the brain and the world — cycling, motorcycle, and industrial helmets — under conditions that reflect how they actually fail.

Where we sit

Imperial College London.

We are part of the Dyson School of Design Engineering at Imperial College London, in South Kensington. The lab is directed by Dr Mazdak Ghajari, Reader (Associate Professor) in Brain Biomechanics, and is funded by UK research councils, road-safety bodies, and industry partners. We collaborate widely across Imperial, the NHS, the FIA, and national governing bodies in sport.

Why it matters

Brain injury is common. Most of it is preventable.

Traumatic brain injury is one of the leading causes of death and long-term disability worldwide. Many of the loading scenarios that cause it — cycling falls, contact-sport collisions, slips on industrial sites, blast exposure in defence settings — are well within reach of engineering. The barrier to better protection has not been physics, it has been the absence of validated brain-tissue-level criteria for the protective devices we already build. Closing that gap is what the lab works on.

How we work

Modelling and physical testing.

Computational models are only as good as the data they are validated against and physical testing only matters if it reflects how injury actually happens. We move between the two, running drop-tower experiments to bound a simulation, simulating real-world scenarios to design the next test — and we publish the underlying methods openly.
